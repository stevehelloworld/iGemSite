<!-- Optimizing Our Engineering Design -->

<section class="ihp-section">

    <div class="section-title">

        <h2>
            Optimizing Our Engineering Design
        </h2>

    </div>

    <p class="section-description">

        After identifying the practical challenges surrounding heavy metal
        remediation, we consulted Professor Hung-Yu Lai from National Chung
        Hsing University, whose research focuses on soil remediation and heavy
        metal management.

        Rather than validating our original design, Professor Lai encouraged us
        to critically evaluate our engineering strategy from the perspective of
        practical implementation.

    </p>



    <!-- =============================== -->

    <div class="design-card">

        <div class="design-header">

            <h3>

                Challenge 1

            </h3>

            <h4>

                Which metallothionein localization strategy is better?

            </h4>

        </div>

        <div class="design-content">

            <div class="design-column">

                <h5>

                    Our Initial Question

                </h5>

                <p>

                    We designed two different metallothionein localization
                    strategies:

                </p>

                <ul>

                    <li>Cell surface display</li>

                    <li>Intracellular accumulation</li>

                </ul>

                <p>

                    We wanted to determine which strategy would provide the best
                    remediation performance.

                </p>

            </div>

            <div class="design-column">

                <h5>

                    Professor Lai's Feedback

                </h5>

                <p>

                    Professor Lai pointed out that both strategies ultimately
                    remove heavy metals from water if the engineered bacteria
                    can later be separated from the solution.

                    Therefore, instead of assuming one design is superior,
                    experimental evidence should determine which performs
                    better.

                </p>

            </div>

            <div class="design-column">

                <h5>

                    Project Improvement

                </h5>

                <ul>

                    <li>

                        Compare both localization strategies experimentally.

                    </li>

                    <li>

                        Evaluate remediation efficiency.

                    </li>

                    <li>

                        Compare sensing performance.

                    </li>

                </ul>

            </div>

        </div>

    </div>





    <!-- =============================== -->

    <div class="design-card">

        <div class="design-header">

            <h3>

                Challenge 2

            </h3>

            <h4>

                Selecting Appropriate Experimental Conditions

            </h4>

        </div>

        <div class="design-content">

            <div class="design-column">

                <h5>

                    Professor Lai's Feedback

                </h5>

                <p>

                    Before comparing engineering strategies,
                    it is essential to determine an appropriate cadmium
                    concentration range.

                    Excessive cadmium concentrations may damage bacterial cells,
                    making subsequent performance comparisons unreliable.

                </p>

            </div>

            <div class="design-column">

                <h5>

                    Project Improvement

                </h5>

                <ul>

                    <li>

                        Test multiple Cd concentration ranges.

                    </li>

                    <li>

                        Determine the optimal operating range.

                    </li>

                    <li>

                        Compare system performance under different conditions.

                    </li>

                </ul>

            </div>

        </div>

    </div>





    <!-- =============================== -->

    <div class="design-card">

        <div class="design-header">

            <h3>

                Challenge 3

            </h3>

            <h4>

                Evaluating Cell Health

            </h4>

        </div>

        <div class="design-content">

            <div class="design-column">

                <h5>

                    Professor Lai's Feedback

                </h5>

                <p>

                    Besides measuring fluorescence,
                    bacterial health should also be evaluated.

                    A sensing system cannot perform reliably if the engineered
                    bacteria lose viability under heavy metal stress.

                </p>

            </div>

            <div class="design-column">

                <h5>

                    Project Improvement

                </h5>

                <ul>

                    <li>

                        Include bacterial viability as an evaluation metric.

                    </li>

                    <li>

                        Monitor cell growth throughout experiments.

                    </li>

                    <li>

                        Evaluate system stability.

                    </li>

                </ul>

            </div>

        </div>

    </div>





    <!-- =============================== -->

    <div class="design-card">

        <div class="design-header">

            <h3>

                Challenge 4

            </h3>

            <h4>

                Future Environmental Applications

            </h4>

        </div>

        <div class="design-content">

            <div class="design-column">

                <h5>

                    Professor Lai's Perspective

                </h5>

                <p>

                    Water and soil are fundamentally different environments.

                    Compared with wastewater,
                    soil contains far more variables,
                    making direct application considerably more challenging.

                </p>

            </div>

            <div class="design-column">

                <h5>

                    Project Improvement

                </h5>

                <ul>

                    <li>

                        Continue focusing on wastewater remediation.

                    </li>

                    <li>

                        Consider soil remediation as a future application.

                    </li>

                    <li>

                        Plan future validation under more complex
                        environmental conditions.

                    </li>

                </ul>

            </div>

        </div>

    </div>

</section>
