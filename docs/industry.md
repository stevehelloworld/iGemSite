        <h2 id="industry-title">Industry Partners: Mega Union</h2>
    </div>

    <div class="industry-hero-visual" aria-hidden="true">
        <img src="/static/assets/images/problem/wastewater-mascot-flat.png" alt="">
        <div><span>FIELD NOTE</span><strong>What industry taught us</strong><small>Real treatment systems have real-world constraints.</small></div>
    </div>

    <p class="section-description">We spoke with Mega Union, a water treatment and resource recovery provider serving high-tech industries such as semiconductor manufacturing. The discussion helped us understand the limits of current heavy-metal treatment and what a biological solution would need to achieve in practice.</p>

    <div class="industry-interview-note"><strong>Interview insight</strong><p>For industrial adoption, reliability and selectivity matter as much as removal efficiency.</p></div>

    <div class="industry-fact-grid">
        <article class="industry-fact-card"><figure class="industry-explainer-image"><img src="/static/assets/images/problem/lead-mascot-flat.png" alt="Lead character representing metal contamination from batteries and soldering"><figcaption>Metal enters wastewater</figcaption></figure><span>01 / WASTEWATER</span><h3>Where metals enter the process</h3><p>Semiconductor and electronics wastewater can contain Lead (Pb) and Chromium (Cr). Lead may come from battery systems and soldering, while Chromium is used in surface treatments for corrosion resistance.</p></article>
        <article class="industry-fact-card"><figure class="industry-explainer-image"><img src="/static/assets/images/problem/wastewater-mascot-flat.png" alt="Water character collecting metal particles during wastewater polishing"><figcaption>Low concentration remains</figcaption></figure><span>02 / TREATMENT</span><h3>Why polishing matters</h3><p>Higher-concentration waste liquid may be returned to suppliers for reprocessing. The remaining low-concentration polishing stage is therefore an important opportunity for a selective biological system.</p></article>
        <article class="industry-fact-card"><figure class="industry-explainer-image"><img src="/static/assets/images/solution/detection-flat.png" alt="Detection illustration representing reliable monitoring in an industrial treatment system"><figcaption>Performance must be measurable</figcaption></figure><span>03 / IMPLEMENTATION</span><h3>What industry needs</h3><p>A future solution must be stable, easy to operate, compatible with existing treatment systems, and practical to scale within limited factory space.</p></article>
    </div>

    <div class="industry-subheading"><span>THE COMPARISON</span><h3>Current treatment and our biological approach</h3></div>
    <div class="industry-compare-grid">
        <article class="industry-compare-card current-method"><span class="industry-card-label">CURRENT INDUSTRIAL METHODS</span><img class="industry-card-illustration" src="/static/assets/images/solution/removal-flat.png" alt=""><h4>Catch, separate, and recover</h4><ul><li>Chemical precipitation turns dissolved metals into hydroxides.</li><li>Chelating agents such as EDTA or BTC bind metal ions.</li><li>Membrane filtration can separate bacteria from water with reported efficiency up to 99.9%.</li></ul></article>
        <div class="industry-compare-arrow" aria-hidden="true">→</div>
        <article class="industry-compare-card biological-method"><span class="industry-card-label">OUR ENGINEERED SYSTEM</span><img class="industry-card-illustration" src="/static/assets/images/solution/capture-flat.png" alt=""><h4>Specific biological “hands”</h4><ul><li>Metallothionein (MT) provides protein domains that bind selected heavy metals.</li><li>Engineered E. coli could complement, rather than replace, established treatment.</li><li>Selectivity may support recovery of a high-purity target metal from mixed ions.</li></ul></article>
    </div>

    <div class="industry-subheading"><span>THE THREE CHALLENGES</span><h3>What must be solved before scale-up?</h3></div>
    <div class="industry-challenge-grid"><article><img src="/static/assets/images/solution/detection-flat.png" alt="Detection illustration representing repeated stability checks"><b>01</b><h4>Stability</h4><p>Industrial plants need predictable performance. An unexpected discharge could exceed limits and create major operational consequences.</p></article><article><img src="/static/assets/images/solution/removal-flat.png" alt="Removal illustration representing compact treatment equipment"><b>02</b><h4>Land footprint</h4><p>Physical-chemical treatment may take about 30 minutes, while biological treatment can take hours or days and may require larger tanks.</p></article><article><img src="/static/assets/images/problem/wastewater-mascot-flat.png" alt="Water character representing bacteria facing harsh wastewater conditions"><b>03</b><h4>Harsh conditions</h4><p>Extreme pH and high conductivity can damage cells. The engineered bacteria must remain resilient in the target wastewater environment.</p></article></div>

    <div class="industry-subheading"><span>THE BIGGER OPPORTUNITY</span><h3>From waste to resource</h3></div>
    <div class="resource-flow" aria-label="Circular economy flow from waste to recovered metal"><div><img class="resource-flow-image" src="/static/assets/images/problem/wastewater-mascot-flat.png" alt="Wastewater containing mixed metal ions"><span>1</span><strong>Wastewater</strong><small>Mixed metal ions enter treatment.</small></div><i aria-hidden="true">→</i><div><img class="resource-flow-image" src="/static/assets/images/solution/capture-flat.png" alt="Metallothionein selectively capturing a target metal"><span>2</span><strong>Selective binding</strong><small>MT captures a chosen target metal.</small></div><i aria-hidden="true">→</i><div><img class="resource-flow-image" src="/static/assets/images/solution/removal-flat.png" alt="Bound metal being separated from treated water"><span>3</span><strong>Separation</strong><small>The bound material is removed from water.</small></div><i aria-hidden="true">→</i><div><img class="resource-flow-image" src="/static/assets/images/solution/clean-water-flat.png" alt="Clean water and recovered metal ready for reuse"><span>4</span><strong>Recovered resource</strong><small>Metal can return to industrial use.</small></div></div>

    <div class="industry-conclusion"><span>WHAT CHANGED IN OUR PROJECT</span><p>We are focusing on the low-concentration polishing stage, where traditional chemicals may struggle, while exploring ways to improve MT selectivity and system reliability for a more sustainable circular economy.</p></div>
