        <div><span>FIELD NOTE</span><strong>What industry taught us</strong><small>Real treatment systems have real-world constraints.</small></div>
    </div>

    <p class="section-description">We spoke with Mega Union, a water treatment and resource recovery provider serving high-tech industries such as semiconductor manufacturing. The discussion helped us identify operational questions for a possible biological approach. These insights are industry context: our lab assays focus on cadmium in model media and have not tested industrial wastewater.</p>

    <div class="industry-interview-note"><strong>Interview insight</strong><p>For industrial adoption, reliability and selectivity matter as much as removal efficiency.</p></div>

    <div class="industry-fact-grid">
        <article class="industry-fact-card"><figure class="industry-explainer-image"><img src="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6423/wiki/static/assets/images/problem/lead-mascot-flat.avif" alt="Character representing metal contamination from batteries and soldering"><figcaption>Metal enters wastewater</figcaption></figure><span>01 / WASTEWATER</span><h3>Where metals enter the process</h3><p>Semiconductor and electronics wastewater can contain Chromium (Cr), used in surface treatments for corrosion resistance, along with other metals from battery systems and soldering.</p></article>
        <article class="industry-fact-card"><figure class="industry-explainer-image"><img src="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6423/wiki/static/assets/images/problem/wastewater-mascot-flat.avif" alt="Water character collecting metal particles during wastewater polishing"><figcaption>Low concentration remains</figcaption></figure><span>02 / TREATMENT</span><h3>Why polishing matters</h3><p>Higher-concentration waste liquid may be returned to suppliers for reprocessing. The remaining low-concentration polishing stage is therefore an important opportunity for a selective biological system.</p></article>
        <article class="industry-fact-card"><figure class="industry-explainer-image"><img src="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6423/wiki/static/assets/images/solution/detection-flat.avif" alt="Detection illustration representing reliable monitoring in an industrial treatment system"><figcaption>Performance must be measurable</figcaption></figure><span>03 / IMPLEMENTATION</span><h3>What industry needs</h3><p>A future solution must be stable, easy to operate, compatible with existing treatment systems, and practical to scale within limited factory space.</p></article>
    </div>

    <div class="industry-subheading"><span>THE THREE CHALLENGES</span><h3>What must be solved before scale-up?</h3></div>
    <div class="industry-challenge-grid"><article><img src="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6423/wiki/static/assets/images/solution/detection-flat.avif" alt="Detection illustration representing repeated stability checks"><b>01</b><h4>Stability</h4><p>A future treatment system would need predictable performance under defined operating conditions. We have not yet tested stability in industrial wastewater.</p></article><article><img src="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6423/wiki/static/assets/images/solution/removal-flat.avif" alt="Removal illustration representing compact treatment equipment"><b>02</b><h4>Residence time and footprint</h4><p>Any proposed biological process would need to be compared with existing methods for treatment time, tank size, and operating cost using the same target conditions.</p></article><article><img src="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6423/wiki/static/assets/images/problem/wastewater-mascot-flat.avif" alt="Water character representing bacteria facing harsh wastewater conditions"><b>03</b><h4>Wastewater conditions</h4><p>pH, conductivity, and other components could affect cell performance. The team's current assays do not establish how the constructs behave in target wastewater.</p></article></div>

    <div class="industry-conclusion"><span>WHAT CHANGED IN OUR PROJECT</span><p>The interview raised low-concentration treatment, reliability, and selectivity as design considerations. Our current work is a laboratory study of cadmium-responsive growth and GFP output; it has not demonstrated wastewater treatment, cadmium removal, or metal recovery.</p></div>
