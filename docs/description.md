<section class="problem-statement-page">
  <div class="problem-statement-hero">
    <span class="problem-eyebrow">Problem Statement</span>
    <h1>Cadmium contamination is a long-term environmental and public health problem.</h1>
    <p>
      Industrial activities release cadmium ions into wastewater, soil, and irrigation systems.
      Because cadmium is persistent and bioaccumulative, even low concentrations can create
      long-lasting risks for ecosystems, agriculture, and human health.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="problem-content-grid">
    <article class="problem-text-card">
      <h2>Cadmium problems</h2>
      <p>
        Cadmium ions (Cd²⁺) can be released through industrial processes such as electroplating,
        metal processing, chemical manufacturing, and wastewater discharge. Once cadmium enters the
        environment, it does not easily degrade or disappear.
      </p>
      <p>
        Cadmium remains in contaminated environments for long periods, making affected soil and water
        difficult to restore. It can accumulate in organisms and move through the food chain, turning
        local pollution into wider ecological and public health concerns.
      </p>
    </article>

    <aside class="problem-highlight-card">
      <span class="problem-highlight-label">Key concern</span>
      <p>
        Cadmium is toxic even at low concentrations and can threaten food safety when contaminated
        water or soil reaches agricultural systems.
      </p>
    </aside>
  </div>

  <div class="problem-standard-band">

  <section class="problem-section">
    <h2>Cadmium contamination in Taiwan is a national problem</h2>
    <p>
      Cadmium pollution is not limited to one isolated location. In Taiwan, several regions have
      experienced serious contamination connected to industrial wastewater and nearby agricultural
      land.
    </p>

    <div class="taiwan-case-grid">
      <article>
        <h3>Changhua County <span class="case-percent">≈ 47%</span></h3>
        <p>
          Electroplating and metal processing wastewater entered irrigation canals, contributing to
          widespread cadmium contamination in agricultural areas.
        </p>
      </article>

      <article>
        <h3>Taoyuan City <span class="case-percent">≈ 46%</span></h3>
        <p>
          Taoyuan is associated with early cadmium rice incidents, where chemical plants discharged
          high-cadmium wastewater into surrounding systems.
        </p>
      </article>

      <article>
        <h3>Taichung City <span class="case-percent">≈ 5%</span></h3>
        <p>
          Metal surface treatment and unregistered factories have contributed to local heavy metal
          contamination risks.
        </p>
      </article>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="problem-section">
    <h2>Current remediation methods still have limitations</h2>
    <div class="remediation-grid">
      <article>
        <h3>Chemical precipitation</h3>
        <p>
          This method can be costly and may generate toxic sludge, creating additional waste handling
          challenges and possible secondary contamination.
        </p>
      </article>

      <article>
        <h3>Ion exchange and adsorption</h3>
        <p>
          These approaches rely on consumable materials. Over time, replacement and regeneration
          costs can become a major barrier for long-term use.
        </p>
      </article>

      <article>
        <h3>Conventional bioremediation</h3>
        <p>
          Traditional biological methods may be less targeted. When cadmium enters cells, it can
          disrupt metabolism and reduce cell survival.
        </p>
      </article>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="problem-solution-need">
    <span>What is needed?</span>

    <p>
      An effective system should selectively detect cadmium, remove it before it enters the cell, and
      remain low-cost and scalable. Our project explores an engineered <em>E. coli</em> surface
      detection and binding strategy to address cadmium contamination in wastewater.
    </p>
